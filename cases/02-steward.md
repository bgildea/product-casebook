# Steward: AI-native SME succession for Southeast Asia

**Role:** Co-founder (with a Thai-licensed lawyer), product lead
**Stage:** Concept, pitch decks and investor research

## Context

A large share of small and medium businesses in Southeast Asia are owned by founders approaching retirement, with no plan for who takes over. Succession is usually handled late, informally, and expensively, through a mix of family conversations, accountants and lawyers.

## Problem

Owners don't start succession planning because it is emotionally hard, legally confusing and has no obvious first step. Advisers are expensive and engage only once a sale or handover is already happening.

## What I did

- Framed the problem as "the first 90 days of succession", not "selling a business", to find where an owner would actually start
- Mapped the journey with my co-founder: valuation questions, family and staff conversations, legal structure, documentation, finding a buyer or successor
- Identified where AI fits and where it doesn't. Good fits: organising scattered company documents, drafting a first-pass information memorandum, answering "what happens next" questions in plain Thai or English. Bad fits: valuation opinions and legal advice, which stay with licensed people
- Built the pitch narrative and researched investors active in Southeast Asian SME and legal-tech

## Decision record: who is the first customer?

| Option | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Owners directly | Biggest market, clearest pain | Hard to reach, low trust in a new brand, long sales cycle |
| B. Accountants and law firms as a channel | They already hold the relationship and the documents | They may see it as competition |
| C. Banks with SME portfolios | Strong incentive to keep loans healthy through a handover | Slow procurement, heavy compliance |

Option B frames Steward as a tool that makes advisers faster and lets them serve smaller clients profitably, rather than a replacement for them.

**Status:** not tested yet. Steward is at concept and pitch stage, so this decision stays open until there is customer evidence to settle it. Writing the options down early means the first conversations with owners, advisers or banks can be aimed at the question that matters most.

## Where AI fits

Keeping AI to organising, drafting and explaining, with licensed people owning valuation and legal advice, is what makes the product credible in a regulated area.

## What I learned

Across this and several other concepts, the same pattern held: ideas grounded in an existing distribution channel beat ideas that needed to build one. That now shapes how I assess any new product bet.

## Skills this shows

Problem framing, journey mapping, deciding where AI belongs in a regulated domain, go-to-market thinking.
